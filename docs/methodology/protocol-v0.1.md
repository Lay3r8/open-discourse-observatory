# Research protocol v0.1

## Research question

Since 2000, do Western democracies show an ideological asymmetry in attempts to sanction, exclude, suppress or physically obstruct political expression, after comparing like with like?

## Initial scope

Pilot countries:

- France
- Belgium
- United Kingdom
- United States
- Canada

Period: 2000-2026.

## Null hypothesis

After accounting for event type, country, period, sector and opportunity to act, there is no systematic left/right asymmetry in frequency, severity or institutional/media response.

## Inclusion principle

Code observable behavior rather than moral labels. An event can qualify when it contains at least one measurable action such as organized pressure on a third party, deplatforming, employment/professional sanction, doxxing, targeted harassment, threat, physical obstruction, violence or legal restriction.

Mere criticism, disagreement or negative commentary is not sufficient by itself.

## Symmetry rules

- Apply the same definitions to every ideological camp.
- Search explicitly for disconfirming cases.
- Compare equivalent action types rather than raw anecdote totals.
- Preserve mixed or uncertain actor classifications.
- Distinguish private activism, employer/institutional action and state coercion.
- Treat same-camp dissent separately from cross-camp conflict.

## Evidence

Prefer, in order where available:

1. court judgments and official records;
2. primary institutional statements;
3. multiple independent high-quality news reports;
4. direct full-length video or documentary records;
5. advocacy sources only with corroboration for disputed facts.

## Automation boundary

Automated systems may discover, normalize, deduplicate and propose classifications. They may not promote a candidate into the canonical `research` schema without human validation.

## Pilot warning

The v0.1 pilot is a method-development sample assembled non-randomly. Its raw ideological counts are not prevalence estimates and must not be quoted as population ratios.
